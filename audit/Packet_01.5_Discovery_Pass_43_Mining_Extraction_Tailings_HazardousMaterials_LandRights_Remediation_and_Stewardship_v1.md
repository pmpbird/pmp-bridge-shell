## Provisional records

### EXTRACT-001 — Resource estimate is treated as certainty

Geology, grade, water, faults, weathering, and extraction conditions may differ from limited sampling and models.

HARM: investment and safety planning depend on a resource body that does not behave as predicted.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: DESIGN-004, MODEL-003.

### EXTRACT-002 — Production pressure changes the extraction sequence

Higher-value zones, easier access, or schedule pressure may reorder work from the validated plan.

HARM: ground stability, ventilation, drainage, and emergency access assumptions become invalid.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: MANUF-001, CHANGE-005.

### EXTRACT-003 — Extraction changes groundwater and surface-water flow beyond the site

Dewatering, blasting, subsidence, tunnels, pits, and waste rock may redirect or contaminate water.

HARM: distant wells, wetlands, rivers, and communities experience delayed effects.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: WATERUTIL-005, ENVRES-001.

### EXTRACT-004 — Remote-operation confidence exceeds physical visibility

Cameras, telemetry, maps, and sensors may miss dust, vibration, loose ground, people, and changing weather.

HARM: operators make high-energy decisions from an incomplete remote picture.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: OT-006, ROBOT-006.

### EXTRACT-005 — Valuable byproduct recovery introduces an unreviewed chemical process

New reagents, heat, pressure, storage, or separation steps may be added after the original design.

HARM: economic optimization creates novel worker and environmental hazards.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: LAB-003, MATERIAL-002.

### EXTRACT-006 — Commodity-price decline shifts risk into deferred safety work

Inspection, maintenance, staffing, monitoring, and waste control may be reduced during low-price periods.

HARM: financial stress becomes latent physical and environmental failure.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: COST-004, MAINT-005.

### TAILING-001 — Tailings stability model omits changing material behavior

Particle size, chemistry, saturation, consolidation, oxidation, and deposition pattern may evolve over time.

HARM: containment assumptions degrade while the structure appears unchanged.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: STRUCT-002, EXP-006.

### TAILING-002 — Water balance is wrong during compound weather events

Rain, snowmelt, upstream inflow, blocked drains, pump loss, and power failure may occur together.

HARM: overtopping or uncontrolled discharge occurs despite ordinary design compliance.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: GRID-001, FIRE-003.

### TAILING-003 — Monitoring instruments move with the failing structure

Piezometers, survey markers, cameras, and communication lines may shift, lose power, or become inaccessible.

HARM: the monitoring system reports less precisely as failure develops.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: SENSE-006, INCDET-001.

### TAILING-004 — Upstream and downstream communities receive different warning time

Alerting may favor the operator, regulator, or nearby settlements while distant populations and language groups remain uninformed.

HARM: the same failure creates unequal survival opportunity.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: TELECOM-005, CIVIC-006.

### TAILING-005 — Closure design assumes indefinite maintenance funding

Pumps, covers, drainage, monitoring, treatment, and repairs may be needed for decades or centuries.

HARM: containment outlives the operator and the financial mechanism supporting it.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: SUCCESS-001, STEWARD-001.

### TAILING-006 — Emergency release path is not modeled for real terrain and debris

Sediment, structures, vegetation, ice, channels, and secondary dams may redirect flow.

HARM: inundation maps understate where and how fast destructive material travels.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: EMROUTE-001, REMSENSE-001.

### HAZMAT-001 — Hazard classification changes after material mixing or weathering

Ore, waste rock, process water, reagents, fuels, and residues may react over time.

HARM: handling rules remain tied to the original material rather than the new hazard.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: MATERIAL-002, CONTAM-002.

### HAZMAT-002 — Dust monitoring underrepresents the worker’s real exposure

Fixed monitors, short samples, calm periods, respirator use, and task variation may miss peak inhalation.

HARM: compliant area measurements coexist with harmful personal exposure.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: WATERUTIL-001, MEAS-002.

### HAZMAT-003 — Hazardous transport route crosses unprepared communities

Fuel, explosives, acids, concentrates, and waste may move through roads, ports, and rail lines lacking emergency capacity.

HARM: off-site populations bear risks they did not choose or plan for.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: LOGISTICS-004, PARTICIP-002.

### HAZMAT-004 — Spill response creates incompatible chemical or environmental reactions

Water, foam, absorbents, neutralizers, heat, and containment materials may worsen some releases.

HARM: generic emergency action expands the hazard.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: CONTAM-003, HAZ-005.

### HAZMAT-005 — Hazard labels and records detach from secondary containers and waste

Transfers, temporary storage, reused drums, weather, and contractor handling may break identity.

HARM: workers and responders encounter unknown material.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: LAB-005, TRACE-002.

### LAND-001 — Land title is treated as the full record of land rights

Customary use, seasonal access, grazing, water, burial, sacred sites, and collective tenure may not appear in formal title.

HARM: legal ownership erases legitimate community relationships to land.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: HERIT-002, PARTICIP-002.

### LAND-002 — Consultation occurs after the project is effectively irreversible

Licenses, financing, roads, contracts, and technical design may be committed before meaningful community choice.

HARM: participation exists procedurally but cannot alter the outcome.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: AUTON-002, CIVIC-003.

### LAND-003 — Compensation measures market price but not livelihood or cultural loss

Relocation may disrupt water, farming, kinship, identity, sacred places, and informal economies.

HARM: payment is treated as complete remedy for nonfinancial harm.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: RECOVER-001, SACRED-001.

### LAND-004 — Boundary and access maps omit changing local use

Routes, gathering areas, migration, seasonal camps, and community growth may shift after baseline surveys.

HARM: later users are classified as encroachers on land they rely upon.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: CIVPROT-001, CULT-005.

### LAND-005 — Security around extraction sites criminalizes ordinary local activity

Fishing, grazing, travel, collecting, protest, photography, and visiting sacred areas may be treated as threats.

HARM: industrial security expands into community policing and exclusion.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: POLICE-003, CONFLICT-004.

### EJ-001 — Environmental burden is concentrated where political resistance is weakest

Waste, dust, noise, traffic, water loss, and accident risk may fall disproportionately on marginalized communities.

HARM: project efficiency depends on unequal ability to refuse harm.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: ALLOC-002, PRICE-002.

### EJ-002 — Benefit accounting counts national revenue while omitting local costs

Taxes, exports, jobs, and infrastructure may be measured separately from displacement, illness, inflation, and ecosystem loss.

HARM: aggregate benefit hides community-level damage.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: SPACE-003, MEAS-001.

### EJ-003 — Community health baseline is too weak to prove later harm

Limited testing, short records, inaccessible care, and underdiagnosis may leave no credible pre-project comparison.

HARM: the absence of baseline evidence protects the polluter rather than the community.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: DESIGN-002, HEALTH-006.

### EJ-004 — Technical risk communication excludes local language and knowledge

Probability, chemistry, geotechnical models, and legal notices may not connect with lived experience or local terms.

HARM: communities cannot meaningfully evaluate or contest the risk.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: LANG-001, NORM-005.

### EJ-005 — Grievance system depends on the operator being challenged

Intake, evidence, investigation, translation, and remedy may be controlled by the company or its contractors.

HARM: affected people must seek justice through the alleged source of harm.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: DUE-004, LIAB-004.

### MINEWORK-001 — Production incentives suppress hazard reporting

Workers may fear lost bonuses, delay, blame, discipline, or job loss when reporting ground, gas, dust, equipment, or fatigue concerns.

HARM: early warning is silenced before a serious incident.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: CARE-006, INCENT-003.

### MINEWORK-002 — Remote-site work magnifies fatigue and medical delay

Long shifts, travel, altitude, heat, isolation, and limited emergency care may combine.

HARM: ordinary impairment becomes a high-consequence operational risk.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: FAT-004, EMSAFE-005.

### MINEWORK-003 — Contractor workers receive weaker training and protection

Temporary, migrant, and subcontracted labor may have less site knowledge, language support, equipment, or reporting power.

HARM: risk is shifted to the least empowered workers.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: BUILDCHAIN-003, CONTRACT-002.

### MINEWORK-004 — Autonomous equipment changes pedestrian and vehicle assumptions

Haul trucks, drills, loaders, and inspection robots may operate with different routes, stopping behavior, and visibility.

HARM: mixed human and autonomous traffic creates novel collision risk.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: ROBOT-002, VEH-001.

### MINEWORK-005 — Rescue plan assumes access remains intact after collapse or fire

Roads, shafts, communications, ventilation, power, and refuge areas may fail together.

HARM: nominal emergency capability cannot reach trapped workers.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: CASCADE-001, FIRE-004.

### CLOSURE-001 — Closure plan is optimized for regulatory sign-off rather than durable stability

Short monitoring periods and visible rehabilitation may omit long-term water, chemistry, subsidence, and ecosystem change.

HARM: formal closure transfers unresolved risk to the public.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: RESTORE-003, TAILING-005.

### CLOSURE-002 — Financial assurance becomes insufficient after cost escalation or operator failure

Inflation, new contamination, technical failure, and bankruptcy may exceed the bond or trust.

HARM: taxpayers inherit remediation costs.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: INSUR-005, SUCCESS-001.

### CLOSURE-003 — Closure removes employment before regional transition exists

Workers, suppliers, housing, schools, and public revenue may depend on one operation.

HARM: environmental closure triggers economic and social collapse.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: RECOVER-003, LABOR-002.

### CLOSURE-004 — Reclaimed land is declared usable before hidden hazards stabilize

Subsidence, acid drainage, dust, unstable slopes, and contaminated soil may reappear later.

HARM: new residents or users are exposed after official completion.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: STRUCT-002, MATERIAL-004.

### CLOSURE-005 — Monitoring access is lost after ownership or land-use change

Property transfer, fencing, redevelopment, and missing easements may block wells, sensors, and inspection routes.

HARM: long-term hazards continue after the ability to observe them disappears.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: GROUND-003, LAND-004.

### STEWARD-001 — Long-term stewardship has no accountable successor

Companies, regulators, trusts, and governments may change or disappear while waste remains hazardous.

HARM: permanent risk outlives temporary institutions.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: SPACE-006, TAILING-005.

### STEWARD-002 — Institutional memory decays across generations

Hazard maps, treatment knowledge, access rules, buried infrastructure, and failure history may be lost or simplified.

HARM: future caretakers unknowingly repeat unsafe actions.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: SUCCESS-001, ARCHIVE-001.

### STEWARD-003 — Warning markers do not remain understandable over long time horizons

Language, symbols, land use, literacy, and cultural meaning may change.

HARM: future people enter or disturb hazardous sites without recognizing the warning.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: LANG-006, SACRED-004.

### STEWARD-004 — Remediation success metric ignores ecological substitution

A site may look green while biodiversity, soil function, hydrology, and cultural use remain degraded.

HARM: visual rehabilitation is mistaken for ecosystem recovery.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: HERIT-004, MEAS-004.

### STEWARD-005 — Treatment system creates an indefinite dependency on energy and chemicals

Pumping, neutralization, filtration, and monitoring may be required continuously.

HARM: a supposedly closed site remains tied to fragile infrastructure forever.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: CASCADE-001, WATERUTIL-004.
