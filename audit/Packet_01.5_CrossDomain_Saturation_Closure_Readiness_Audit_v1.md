## Findings

### Finding 1 — Diminishing-yield requirement: PASS

Six unrestricted passes produced a continuous decline in natural yield while duplicate pressure rose from high to extreme and slowdown reached near-exhaustion.

This is sustained evidence that new broad candidates are becoming progressively harder to separate from the existing register.

### Finding 2 — Independent challenge requirement: PASS

The receiver-style inversion used a different search direction and still found no new major root family.

Most generated candidates were rejected as semantic repeats before entry.

### Finding 3 — Major-domain completeness at exercised scope: PASS

No known material major root-system family remains missing or materially partial.

This does not mean every industry or future product workflow has a dedicated pass. Those narrower areas remain under the reopening gate.

### Finding 4 — Applicability gate: PASS WITH PERMANENT CONDITION

The register contains many conditional future-domain risks.

They must never be represented as current project defects merely because they were discovered.

Every later routing decision must preserve one of these applicability states:

- CURRENT DEFECT
- ACTIVE CONDITIONAL RISK
- DORMANT FUTURE RISK
- OUT-OF-SCOPE CANDIDATE

A candidate may become an active project requirement only when a demonstrated current or planned project contact path exists.

### Finding 5 — Another automatic discovery pass: NOT JUSTIFIED

A Pass 70 would continue searching after:

- full major-root coverage;
- six strictly declining unrestricted yields;
- extreme duplicate pressure;
- near-exhaustion slowdown;
- a negative-space challenge;
- an independent receiver-style inversion;
- no new major root-system family.

Continuing automatically would now create more inflation risk than discovery value.

## Decision

Packet 01.5 broad discovery is certified saturated **at the exercised major-root and cross-domain scope**.

Automatic discovery passes stop here.

Discovery is not permanently sealed. It moves to an event-triggered reopening model.

Routing preparation is authorized, but routing itself has not started.

No provisional record is merged, deleted, assigned, or individually closed by this audit.

## Reopening gate

Discovery must reopen when at least one of the following occurs:

1. a new current or planned PMP Current/Resident capability enters a sector on the conditional watch list;
2. routing reveals a candidate that cannot fit any established root family without distortion;
3. runtime evidence exposes a materially new failure mechanism;
4. a platform, legal, operational, or ownership change creates a new project contact path;
5. a later breaker or proof run demonstrates that an established family is materially incomplete;
6. a new candidate survives receiver-style comparison against the full register as a distinct high-value structure.

A reopened pass must remain natural-yield, preserve the applicability gate, and state the exact trigger that reopened discovery.

## Routing-preparation boundary

The next phase may prepare routing by:

- preserving every historical record and original wording;
- establishing stable composite addresses using pass number plus record identifier;
- mechanically verifying the final discovery count;
- defining semantic comparison without performing destructive deduplication;
- mapping applicability state separately from destination packet;
- defining owner, secondary owner, cross-cutting law, watch, and no-current-contact destinations;
- designing proof that no record is lost during normalization.

The next phase must not begin assigning or closing records until its preparation gate is independently verified.
