This pass looks for employment and labor-displacement failure, hiring and evaluation bias, worker surveillance, workplace-authority confusion, contractor dependence, job-skill erosion, and employment-record privacy.

## Provisional records

### LABOR-001 — Automation benefit and displacement harm are measured separately

Productivity, cost, speed, and quality may be reported without tracking lost hours, reduced roles, wage pressure, or involuntary reassignment.

HARM: project success is declared while labor harm remains outside the metric.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: MEAS-001, ECO-003.

### LABOR-002 — Task automation removes entry-level learning paths

Routine research, drafting, coding, review, support, and administration may disappear before workers can use them to build judgment.

HARM: future senior capability declines because foundational practice is removed.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: SKILL-001, TEACH-004.

### LABOR-003 — Remaining workers inherit denser and riskier work

Automation may remove easy tasks while leaving exceptions, emergencies, emotional labor, and high-consequence decisions.

HARM: workload appears smaller while cognitive and safety burden rises.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: COG-001, FAT-001.

### LABOR-004 — Productivity gains are converted into workload growth

Faster completion may produce higher quotas, shorter deadlines, fewer staff, and constant availability rather than reduced burden.

HARM: efficiency increases worker pressure instead of improving conditions.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: REBOUND-001, DEAD-001.

### LABOR-005 — Displacement risk falls unevenly across worker groups

Contractors, junior staff, disabled workers, caregivers, remote workers, non-native speakers, and lower-paid roles may be affected first.

HARM: aggregate efficiency hides concentrated harm and exclusion.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: ASSESS-002, EDACCESS-001.

### LABOR-006 — Human fallback capacity disappears after automation adoption

Staffing, procedures, relationships, and manual practice may be reduced because automated operation usually succeeds.

HARM: provider outage or model failure leaves no competent human recovery path.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: SKILL-004, CONT-001.

### HIRE-001 — Hiring model learns historical discrimination

Past selections, performance ratings, retention, and promotion data may encode unequal opportunity and biased management decisions.

HARM: automation reproduces prior discrimination as neutral prediction.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: EVAL-003, KB-002.

### HIRE-002 — Proxy variables reveal protected traits

Location, school, employment gap, name, language, schedule, device, network, and writing style may correlate with protected characteristics.

HARM: prohibited distinctions re-enter through indirect features.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: INFER-001, PRIV-002.

### HIRE-003 — Resume optimization rewards tool access and coaching

Applicants with AI, professional editing, paid templates, and stronger networks may produce more machine-readable applications.

HARM: screening measures preparation resources rather than job ability.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: ASSESS-001, EDACCESS-001.

### HIRE-004 — Automated screening rejects unconventional but qualified candidates

Career changes, disability accommodations, informal experience, nonstandard education, portfolios, and local knowledge may not fit expected patterns.

HARM: valid capability is excluded because it lacks familiar signals.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: ASSESS-004, RANK-004.

### HIRE-005 — Candidate cannot inspect or challenge the hiring basis

Scores, rankings, flags, extracted traits, and rejection reasons may be hidden behind vendor or model systems.

HARM: incorrect or discriminatory decisions become unappealable.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: PRIVRIGHT-005, MOD-004.

### HIRE-006 — Interview analysis infers unsupported personal traits

Voice, facial movement, eye contact, emotion, confidence, honesty, and personality may be inferred from noisy or culturally variable signals.

HARM: pseudoscientific interpretation affects employment opportunity.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: MEDINT-002, SENSE-003.

### HIRE-007 — Candidate data is reused beyond the application

Resumes, recordings, assessments, references, and background information may enter future hiring pools, model training, or unrelated analytics.

HARM: sensitive employment data persists without meaningful consent.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: CONSENT-003, RECORDLAW-002.

### WKEVAL-001 — Performance metric captures visibility rather than contribution

Message volume, online presence, ticket count, response speed, code quantity, and meeting activity may outweigh quiet, preventive, or collaborative work.

HARM: workers optimize measurable activity instead of real value.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: MEAS-001, ASSESS-003.

### WKEVAL-002 — AI-assisted output is attributed entirely to the worker

Quality, speed, errors, and policy violations may reflect model behavior, tool access, prompt design, or provider variation.

HARM: praise and discipline attach to the wrong source of performance.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: COST-001, PROV-003.

### WKEVAL-003 — Comparative ranking creates artificial scarcity

Forced distributions and relative scores may require low performers even when the team meets standards.

HARM: collaboration declines and valid performance is punished.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: RANK-001, FRAME-003.

### WKEVAL-004 — Temporary personal conditions become permanent employment records

Illness, caregiving, crisis, disability flare, network outage, or short-term stress may lower metrics during one period.

HARM: transient conditions create durable career harm.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: ASSESS-007, HEALTH-005.

### WKEVAL-005 — Worker cannot reproduce the evidence behind evaluation

Proprietary models, changed dashboards, hidden weights, private manager notes, and deleted logs may determine outcomes.

HARM: evaluation cannot be audited or contested.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: PROOFCHAIN-008, PRIVRIGHT-005.

### WKEVAL-006 — Evaluation feedback changes behavior in harmful ways

Workers may become risk-averse, hide mistakes, avoid helping others, work while ill, or manipulate metrics.

HARM: the measurement system degrades safety and trust.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: FEED-005, STRESS-004.

### SURV-001 — Workplace monitoring exceeds the work purpose

Keystrokes, screenshots, camera, microphone, location, messages, browsing, biometrics, and device data may be collected continuously.

HARM: employment authority expands into pervasive personal surveillance.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: MOD-005, USERPRIV-004.

### SURV-002 — Consent is invalid under employment pressure

Workers may technically agree to monitoring, AI analysis, or data reuse while refusal threatens employment or opportunity.

HARM: formal consent does not represent free choice.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: CONSENT-005, WKAUTH-001.

### SURV-003 — Remote-work monitoring captures household members and private spaces

Camera, microphone, screen, network, and location tools may collect family, medical, financial, and home information.

HARM: employment surveillance reaches nonemployees who never consented.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: MIC-003, CAM-004.

### SURV-004 — Monitoring data is repurposed for discipline or hiring

Security, productivity, attendance, wellness, and support telemetry may later influence promotion, termination, insurance, or legal disputes.

HARM: data collected for one purpose becomes hidden employment evidence.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: CONSENT-001, HIRE-007.

### SURV-005 — Surveillance system creates a false impression of completeness

Offline work, planning, mentoring, thinking, field activity, and uninstrumented tasks may be invisible.

HARM: unmeasured work is treated as not performed.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: OBS-003, WKEVAL-001.

### SURV-006 — Surveillance data creates safety and retaliation risk

Location, schedule, communications, health patterns, union activity, complaints, and workplace relationships may be exposed to managers or attackers.

HARM: monitoring enables retaliation, stalking, discrimination, or coercion.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: PRIV-002, HARASS-001.

### WKAUTH-001 — Worker cannot distinguish optional guidance from mandatory direction

AI suggestions, manager prompts, dashboards, policy notices, and automated reminders may carry unclear authority.

HARM: workers follow recommendations as orders or ignore actual requirements.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: AUTH-006, DOC-005.

### WKAUTH-002 — Manager delegates consequential decisions to an opaque system

Scheduling, discipline, workload, promotion, termination, and safety decisions may be justified as “the system’s result.”

HARM: human authority remains while accountability is displaced.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: TEACH-003, PRIVRIGHT-005.

### WKAUTH-003 — Worker is held responsible for actions they lacked authority to refuse

Automated procedures or manager instructions may require unsafe, unlawful, deceptive, or privacy-invasive behavior.

HARM: accountability falls on the least powerful actor.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: AUTH-007, STRESS-002.

### WKAUTH-004 — Emergency override power is unclear in the workplace

Workers may not know who can stop automation, suspend production, disclose an incident, evacuate, or reject unsafe output.

HARM: dangerous operation continues during authority confusion.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: AUTH-005, EMSAFE-003.

### WKAUTH-005 — Automated instruction bypasses labor agreement or policy

Scheduling, break timing, task assignment, evaluation, and surveillance may change through software without negotiated review.

HARM: code becomes an undeclared workplace rulemaker.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: REMOTE-001, TERMS-001.

### CONTRACT-001 — Contractor performs core work without continuity protection

Critical knowledge, credentials, code, provider access, and procedures may reside with a temporary external worker.

HARM: departure or dispute removes essential capability.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: HANDOFF-005, CONT-002.

### CONTRACT-002 — Contractor classification hides employer-like control

The organization may dictate tools, schedule, methods, exclusivity, monitoring, and performance while treating the worker as independent.

HARM: obligations and protections are avoided through labeling.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: LEGAL-001, WKAUTH-001.

### CONTRACT-003 — Vendor subcontracting is invisible

A contractor or platform may pass work and data to additional people, regions, models, or services.

HARM: access and labor conditions extend beyond the reviewed party.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: PROV-002, DELEG-002.

### CONTRACT-004 — Contractor incentives conflict with long-term project quality

Payment by speed, ticket, output, milestone, or short engagement may discourage documentation, maintainability, testing, and disclosure of uncertainty.

HARM: short-term delivery creates hidden future cost and fragility.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: DEAD-005, COST-006.

### CONTRACT-005 — Contractor offboarding removes access too early or too late

Immediate revocation may strand handoff work, while delayed revocation leaves unnecessary authority.

HARM: security and continuity requirements conflict without a staged plan.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: REVOKE-003, DELEG-004.

### JOBSKILL-001 — Automation weakens manual job competence

Workers may stop practicing diagnosis, drafting, calculation, coding, navigation, communication, or recovery tasks.

HARM: independent capability decays while output remains high.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: SKILL-004, LABOR-006.

### JOBSKILL-002 — Workers learn to operate the tool but not verify it

Training may emphasize prompts, workflows, dashboards, and acceptance rather than domain reasoning and error detection.

HARM: workers become efficient conduits for undetected mistakes.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: SKILL-002, SKILL-003.

### JOBSKILL-003 — Expert judgment is extracted without preserving expert authority

Experienced workers may supply examples, corrections, and rules that improve automation while their role is reduced.

HARM: the system captures knowledge but discards the people needed to interpret exceptions.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: OWN-003, LABOR-002.

### JOBSKILL-004 — Deskilling is discovered only during failure

Routine automation may conceal competence loss until outage, novel conditions, legal conflict, or emergency response.

HARM: the need for human skill appears after it can no longer be supplied.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: REC-003, CONT-001.

### EMPREC-001 — Employment record contains inferred facts as though observed

Productivity, sentiment, engagement, integrity, health, risk, and intent may be model-generated rather than directly documented.

HARM: speculative judgments become durable personnel facts.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: INFER-001, WKEVAL-005.

### EMPREC-002 — Correction does not propagate across employment systems

Fixes to attendance, performance, identity, accommodation, discipline, or pay may not reach vendors, models, background checks, and archived records.

HARM: corrected errors continue affecting employment decisions.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: PRIVRIGHT-002, SYNC-003.

### EMPREC-003 — Retention of employment data exceeds necessity

Applications, interviews, monitoring, evaluations, health details, complaints, and termination records may remain indefinitely.

HARM: old employment information creates continuing privacy and discrimination risk.

OVERLAP TO CHECK: RECORDLAW-002, HIRE-007.
